Certified Krasikov–Lagarias predecessor bounds for the 3x+1 problem to k = 22: two-sided brackets, machine-checked instances, and the finite-level loss

Draft 0.2, 2026-10-08. Entries marked [TBD] are being produced by runs in progress (Lean k = 19, 20; the k = 22 infeasibility certificate). All other numbers are taken from RESULTS.md, certs/*/cert.json, AVENUES.md, spectrum.json, kl5.json and the literature survey reports/Collatz adjacent work and references.md.

Abstract

For a positive integer a ≢ 0 (mod 3), let π_a(x) be the number of n ≤ x whose 3x+1 orbit passes through a. Krasikov and Lagarias (Acta Arith. 109, 2003) proved π_a(x) ≥ x^{0.84} for x ≥ x₀(a) from a feasible point of a linear program L^NT_k(λ) on the residue classes modulo 3^k, at k = 11. We carry the computation to k = 22, where the program has 3^{21} ≈ 1.05·10^{10} principal variables, and obtain

π_a(x) ≥ x^γ for every γ < 0.92509 and x ≥ x₀(a, γ).

Every level k = 12, …, 22 comes with an integer certificate, the float32 solution vector read as an exact integer vector, verified in int64 arithmetic by two programs that share no code; the verification of the k = 22 certificate (39 GB) takes four minutes. For k = 2, …, 22 we also give infeasibility certificates, positive vectors C with F(C) < C strictly for the right-hand side map F, which prove that the program at level k is infeasible slightly above the certified λ; the Krasikov–Lagarias constants λ_k are thereby bracketed to widths between 2·10^{−5} and 10^{−4}, confirming their numerical values to four or five decimals. The levels k = 18 and 19 [and 20: TBD] are machine-checked in Lean 4 inside L. Mazur’s formalisation of the Krasikov–Lagarias argument, giving theorems x^{0.903} ≤ π_a(x), x^{0.909} ≤ π_a(x) [and x^{0.915} ≤ π_a(x)] for large x, each resting on Lean’s three standard axioms and two compiled finite checks. The question whether λ_k → 2, equivalent to π_a(x) ≥ x^{1−ε} for every ε, reduces to whether a single number, the mean loss ρ_k from the minimum over three lifts, tends to 1. We report its values to k = 22, the spectral radius at λ = 2, a renormalisation argument that predicts geometric convergence, the first spectrum of the linearised transfer matrix (its gap does not close with k), and a calibration run of the same construction on 5x+1. The data to k = 22 are consistent with λ_k → 2 but do not establish it; we state what a proof would need.

1. Introduction

The 3x+1 map is T(n) = n/2 for even n and (3n+1)/2 for odd n. The Collatz conjecture asserts that every orbit reaches 1. The predecessor set of a is {n : T^j(n) = a for some j ≥ 0}; its counting function π_a(x) measures how much of [1, x] lies in the tree below a, and the conjecture is equivalent to π_1(x) = ⌊x⌋.

Lower bounds of the form π_a(x) ≥ x^γ have a forty-year history. Crandall (1978) showed some γ > 0 exists; Sander (1990) reached 3/10; Krasikov (1989) introduced difference inequalities on residue classes modulo 3^k and obtained 3/7; Wirsching (1993) 0.48; Applegate and Lagarias (1995) 0.65 by exhaustive tree search to depth 30 and 0.809 by a nonlinear program on about 20 000 variables; and Krasikov and Lagarias (2003) 0.84, from a linear program with 3^{10} principal variables. Lagarias’s 2010 overview lists 0.84 as the record and states as open that π_1(x) > c(ε) x^{1−ε} for every ε > 0. Krasikov and Lagarias observe in their §6 that their constants λ_k satisfy λ_k ≤ λ_{k+1} and that λ_k → 2 would give the x^{1−ε} bound; no progress on that has appeared.

Three things changed between 2003 and 2026, and this paper uses all three.

Memory. The level-k program has N = 3^{k−1} principal variables and a right-hand side map that needs no stored index arrays: all index maps are affine in the residue. A float32 vector at k = 22 is 39 GB, which a single workstation with unified memory holds, and the map evaluates in seconds on its GPU.

Certification. A solution found in floating point is turned into an integer certificate and checked in exact integer arithmetic; the mathematics of the bound is the Krasikov–Lagarias theorem, and the computation reduces to a check that anyone can re-run in minutes. We add the reverse certificate: because the right-hand side map is monotone and 1-homogeneous, a vector with F(C) < C strictly proves infeasibility, so λ_k becomes a two-sided number.

Formalisation. Mazur’s 2026 Lean 4 development replaces the analytic step of Krasikov–Lagarias (their Theorem 2.2) by a second finite certificate, an “adaptive forced potential” on the same residue classes, and checks both certificates by compiled evaluation (native_decide), so that the statement for a given k is a Lean theorem. We feed our certificates through that development for k = 18, 19 [and 20].

Related work, 2024–2026. Several AI-assisted Lean projects pursue the same program; none is refereed. msharpe248 obtains π_1(x) = Ω(x^{0.8676}) at k = 14 with a kernel-checked certificate (29 h, 37 GB) and 0.895 at k = 17 informally; Mazur obtains 0.90 and 0.901 at k = 18 via native_decide; Menta2357 reproduces the k = 11 value 0.8418 kernel-clean. At equal k these agree with ours to the precision of their respective roundings (msharpe248’s values are lower at equal k because of an integer-grid weakening of the weights). On the cycle side, Barina’s verification of convergence for all n < 2^{71} (2025) makes Hercher’s conditional result unconditional: a nontrivial cycle has at least 1.375·10^{11} odd terms. Our survey of the primary sources, with provenance for every number, is in the repository.

2. The Krasikov–Lagarias system

2.1 Difference inequalities

Fix k ≥ 2. A residue class m mod 3^k with m ≡ 2 (mod 3) is principal: its members have two predecessors under T, the even predecessor 2m and the odd predecessor (2m − 1)/3. Classes ≡ 1 (mod 3) have only the even predecessor, and classes ≡ 0 (mod 3) have only the chain {2^j m}, which contributes O(log x) to any count and is dropped. Writing φ_m(y) for the infimum over a in class m of the number of predecessors of a not exceeding 2^y a, the tree structure gives φ_m(y) ≥ φ_{2m}(y − 1) + φ_{(2m−1)/3}(y − 1 + α) with α = log₂ 3, and eliminating the non-principal classes yields, for each principal m,

  • m ≡ 2 (mod 9): φ_m(y) ≥ φ_{4m}(y − 2) + φ̄_{(4m−2)/3}(y + α − 2),
  • m ≡ 5 (mod 9): φ_m(y) ≥ φ_{4m}(y − 2),
  • m ≡ 8 (mod 9): φ_m(y) ≥ φ_{4m}(y − 2) + φ̄_{(2m−1)/3}(y + α − 1),

where φ̄_r is the infimum over the three classes mod 3^k lying above the class r mod 3^{k−1}, because the odd predecessor is known only modulo 3^{k−1}. (The paper’s equations (2.13)–(2.14) print 3^k where 3^{k−1} is meant; the reproduction of their Table 2 to seven decimals, §3.3, confirms the correction.)

2.2 The linear program and its map

The ansatz φ_m(y) ≥ c_m λ^y turns the inequalities into the linear system L^NT_k(λ): for principal m,

  • m ≡ 2 (mod 9): c_m ≤ λ^{−2} c_{4m} + λ^{α−2} c̄_{(4m−2)/3},
  • m ≡ 5 (mod 9): c_m ≤ λ^{−2} c_{4m},
  • m ≡ 8 (mod 9): c_m ≤ λ^{−2} c_{4m} + λ^{α−1} c̄_{(2m−1)/3},
  • c_m ≥ 1, with c̄r = min(c_r, c{r+3^{k−1}}, c_{r+2·3^{k−1}}).

Theorem (Krasikov–Lagarias, Thm 2.2 and §6). If L^NT_k(λ) is feasible then for every a ≢ 0 (mod 3) and every γ < log₂ λ there is x₀(a, γ) with π_a(x) ≥ x^γ for x ≥ x₀. The constant is explicit: φ_m(y) ≥ Δ₁ c_m λ^y with Δ₁ = 1/(4 max_m c_m).

Let F_λ be the right-hand side map, F(c)m = λ^{−2} c{4m} + λ^{α−j} c̄_{r(m)}. F is monotone and positively 1-homogeneous on the positive cone, so it has a cone spectral radius r(F_λ) (Collatz–Wielandt), and L^NT_k(λ) is feasible iff r(F_λ) ≥ 1: a feasible c satisfies c ≤ F(c), which forces r ≥ 1, and conversely an eigenvector with F(c) = rc, r ≥ 1, is feasible after scaling. Since r is decreasing in λ, λ_k := sup{λ : L^NT_k(λ) feasible} is the unique root of log r(F_λ) = 0.

2.3 Indexing

With N = 3^{k−1} and index i ∈ [0, N) for m = 3i + 2, every map is affine: 4m ↦ (4i + 2) mod N; the class of m mod 9 is i mod 3 (0 ↔ 2, 1 ↔ 5, 2 ↔ 8); the auxiliary index is 4j mod N/3 for i = 3j and (2j + 1) mod N/3 for i = 3j + 2; and c̄[r] = min(c[r], c[r + N/3], c[r + 2N/3]). The ternary digits of i are the digits of m above the lowest, and the top digit is the one the minimum ranges over. Lifting a level-(k−1) vector to level k is c_k[i + j N_{k−1}] = c_{k−1}[i], which preserves feasibility (this is the monotonicity λ_k ≤ λ_{k+1}).

3. Computation

3.1 Power iteration and secant search

We compute λ_k by a secant method on λ ↦ log r(F_λ), with r estimated as the midpoint of the Collatz–Wielandt bounds min_i F(c)_i/c_i ≤ r ≤ max_i F(c)_i/c_i after power iteration c ← F(c)/min F(c). Each λ evaluation is warm-started from the previous iterate, and each level from the lifted solution of the level below; three to five λ values suffice. The secant stops when its step falls below the noise level (hi − lo)/(2|slope|), which is where float32 stops determining λ_k; the slope d log r/dλ ≈ −0.27 means a gap of 10^{−6} in r leaves λ undetermined to about 4·10^{−6}. Certified λ values are rounded down to four decimals, well inside the noise.

3.2 Memory

F is one Metal kernel (Apple MLX): each thread computes its indices in 64-bit integers, reads three lifts and one quadruple index, and writes one output; no index arrays exist. The normalisation 1/min c is folded into the coefficients (F is 1-homogeneous), so the iteration holds two vectors. The Collatz–Wielandt bounds are computed in chunks. For k ≥ 21 the vector exceeds 2^{31} entries and is stored with shape (3, N/3) because MLX shapes are 32-bit.

When two vectors do not fit (k = 21 on 24 GB, k = 22 on 64 GB) the update is compressed: a kernel computes ρ_i = F(c)_i/c_i exactly in float32 together with per-threadgroup minima and maxima, stores d_i = round((ρ_i − ρ₀)/S) as int8 with ρ₀ and S from the previous iteration’s bounds (S = 1.25(hi − lo)/254), and the update c ← (c/m)(ρ₀ + S d) reuses c’s buffer. The quantisation perturbs the iterate by at most S/2, which is irrelevant because certification checks the final vector exactly; it raises the floor of hi − lo to about 2·10^{−6}. Peak memory is 1.26 vectors.

At k = 22 the vector (39.0 GB) exceeds Metal’s single-buffer limit (38.9 GiB on the M4 Pro). We hold it as three buffers indexed by the top ternary digit, c[i] = c_j[i − j N/3]; the lift minimum becomes min(c₀[r], c₁[r], c₂[r]) and the warm start from level 21 is three copies of that vector. The split kernel agrees with the single-buffer kernel bit for bit at k ≤ 10.

4. Certificates

4.1 Feasibility certificates

Let c be the float32 vector after power iteration at λ = P/Q. With b = 23 − ⌊log₂ min c⌋, every C_i = c_i 2^b is an exact integer for every positive normal float32 c_i ≥ min c. Choose D = 2^d with d such that max C · D < 2^{63} and max C · (q + max L_j) < 2^{63}, proved in Python integers before the check; set q = ⌊D Q²/P²⌋ and L_j = ⌊D ℓ_j⌋ where ℓ_j is a rigorous rational lower bound for λ^{α−j} from mpmath interval arithmetic at 200 bits. Then check, for every i, in int64,

C_i D ≤ q C_{(4i+2) mod N} + L_j C̄_{r(i)}.

Since q/D ≤ λ^{−2}, L_j/D ≤ λ^{α−j} and all quantities are nonnegative, the inequality implies C_i ≤ λ^{−2} C_{4m} + λ^{α−j} C̄, so C is feasible for L^NT_k(λ) (C ≥ 1 by homogeneity). No rounding occurs anywhere; the only floating-point object is the input vector, whose bits define C.

Two programs perform this check. certify_fast.py forms C by bit operations (mantissa shifted by the exponent) and streams over chunks in NumPy or on the GPU in int64 (MLX int64 was verified bitwise against NumPy). verify_cert.py uses only NumPy and mpmath, forms C as float64(c)·2^b followed by an exact integrality check, recomputes the interval bounds, re-derives the overflow conditions, and checks the sha256 of the vector against the certificate’s metadata. A --exact mode re-checks the same integers with Python integers for small k. The certificates are rejected when tampered with (an altered entry, q + 1, L + 1, λ raised by 10^{−4}). The check at k = 22 takes 290 s and 4 min respectively.

4.2 Infeasibility certificates

Lemma. Let F be monotone and positively 1-homogeneous on the positive cone of R^N and let C > 0 satisfy F(C)_i < C_i for every i. Then no d ≥ 1 satisfies d ≤ F(d).

Proof. Suppose d ≥ 1 with d ≤ F(d). Let s = max_i d_i/C_i, attained at i. Then d ≤ sC, so by monotonicity and homogeneity d_{i} ≤ F(d){i} ≤ s F(C)_{i} < s C{i} = d_{i}, a contradiction. ∎

An infeasibility certificate is therefore a vector C with the coefficients rounded the other way: q = ⌈D Q²/P²⌉, L_j = ⌈D u_j⌉ with u_j a rigorous rational upper bound for λ^{α−j}, and the strict reverse inequality q C_{4m} + L_j C̄ < D C_i checked in int64 for every i. The power-iterated vector at λ_k + 10^{−5} serves directly (its maximal ratio is about 1 − 3·10^{−6}); at k = 3 the float32 rounding floor of the ratio gap (≈ 3·10^{−5} at N = 9) required λ_k + 2·10^{−4}, and at k = 22 the int8 path’s floor required λ_k + 2·10^{−5} (1.89891; slack 1.6·10^{−6}, 2252 s to re-tune and 293 s to check). 4.3 Certificate metadata

Each cert.json records k, N, λ = P/Q, D, q, L₂, L₁, b, the sha256 of the vector data and of the file, the minimum relative slack, the software versions and the claim; the vectors (1 GB at k = 18 to 39 GB at k = 22) are regenerated by the scripts or available on request. Minimal relative slacks are 6.7·10^{−6} (k = 18), 3.1·10^{−6} (19), 1.4·10^{−6} (20), 1.1·10^{−5} (21), 2.4·10^{−5} (22); slack of order 10^{−7} is the float32 limit, so k ≥ 23 would need float64 polishing or a λ_cert further below λ_k.

5. Formal verification

5.1 Mazur’s development

The Lean development collatz_predecessor_090 (ProofAtlas, July 2026; Lean v4.30.0-rc2, Mathlib pinned) proves, for a weights certificate W: [N) → u32 and a forced-potential certificate P: [N) → u8 on the level-k principal classes, the statement

∀ a > 0, a ≢ 0 (mod 3): x^{γ} ≤ π_a(x) for all sufficiently large real x,

with γ = p/q given and λ = 2^{p/q}. W must satisfy scale · W_i ≤ A · W_{4i} + B_j · min(lifts) for all i, with scale = 2^{28} and A, B_j dyadic lower bounds for λ^{−2}, λ^{α−2}, λ^{α−1} computed by exact integer root extraction (his kl_predecessor_common.py); this is our row check with the same index formulas. P must satisfy P[4i + 2] ≤ P[i] + 6, min over lifts of P at the D1 auxiliary index ≤ P[3q] + 1, and min over lifts at the D3 auxiliary index ≤ P[3q + 2] − 2; this replaces the Kőnig-type elimination in Krasikov–Lagarias’s Theorem 2.2 by a finite object (his AdaptiveCriticalChoice.exponential_lower_bound). P depends on k only. Both certificates are embedded as hexadecimal chunks of 32 MiB (include_str) and checked by native_decide.

5.2 Our additions

  • The weights are our vector rescaled to u32 with minimum 2^{20} (2^{19} at k = 20, where C_max/C_min = 4231 would overflow 32 bits at 2^{20}; the row check is scale-invariant). Before export the vector is re-tuned at the target λ = 2^{p/q} by power iteration, because a vector feasible at λ₁ is not automatically feasible at λ₂ < λ₁: λ^{α−1} decreases with λ and the m ≡ 8 (mod 9) rows can fail (our k = 18 vector at 1.8703 failed 34.8 million rows at 2^{0.903}).
  • The potential is computed as the least fixed point of the constraint system by chunked NumPy (23–26 iterations, 15 s at k = 18 to 4 min at k = 20); it reproduces Mazur’s k = 18 payload bit for bit (sha256 778584ce…).
  • LoopCheck.lean replaces his list-based checker by a tail-recursive loop, proved equivalent to his Valid, which removes a List.range N (16 B per class) from the peak and makes k = 19 and 20 feasible on 64 GB.
  • The level-specific modules K18, K19, K20 (principal index, power-of-two targets, source bridge, coefficient bridge, critical choice, predecessor bridge, all-targets) are generated from his K18 files by textual substitution; the generated certificate modules embed no hashes, only the chunk list, so a regenerated vector needs no Lean changes.

5.4 Trust

native_decide places the Lean compiler in the trust base. A kernel soundness bug (Lean issue #14576, July 2026, fixed in 4.32.2) was exposed by an AI-assisted “disproof” of the Collatz conjecture; we therefore report the axiom audit of every theorem, keep both external verifiers in the loop, and recommend re-running the builds on a toolchain at or above 4.32.2. The memory of native_decide scales linearly with N (about 8–24 bytes per class), so k = 21 and 22 (estimated 90 and 270 GiB) are out of reach of this route on 64 GB; a streamed verified checker in the style of cake_lpr or lrat-catcher, proved once and run natively in constant memory, is the natural next step and would also move the compiled code out of the trust base of each individual certificate.

6. The finite-level loss and the question λ_k → 2

6.1 One number

Summing the inequalities over all principal classes, and using that m ↦ 4m is a bijection of the principal classes and that each auxiliary map is a bijection from a third of them onto the auxiliary classes, gives

(1 − λ^{−2}) c̄{k,k} ≤ (1/3)(λ^{α−1} + λ^{α−2}) c̄{k−1,k},

with c̄{k,k} the mean of c and c̄{k−1,k} the mean of c̄. Write ρ_k = c̄{k−1,k}/c̄{k,k} ≤ 1 for the mean loss from the minimum over lifts. At λ = 2 both sides equal 3/4 when ρ = 1, so λ_k → 2 ⇔ ρ_k → 1. On all our solutions the summed inequality is tight to 10^{−5} (the difference is λ_cert < λ_k), so λ_k is, to that precision, a function of ρ_k alone: 1 − λ^{−2} = (1/3)(λ^{α−1} + λ^{α−2}) ρ.

6.2 The λ = 2 operator

At λ = 2 the same map F₂ has spectral radius r_k < 1, non-decreasing in k with limit r∞ ≤ 1, and λ_k → 2 ⇔ r∞ = 1. The exact identity r_k = 1/4 + (3/4)ρ′_k holds, where ρ′_k is the mixing ratio of the λ = 2 Perron vector itself (checked to 7 decimals at every k up to 22). The sequence 1 − r_k for k = 5 … 22 shrinks by ratios rising from 0.86 to 0.924; at k = 22, r_22 = 0.974971 and ρ′_22 = 0.966628. Geometric-with-offset (limit 1 − r∞ = 0.0072) and stretched-exponential (limit 0) fits reach maximal residual 1.8·10^{−4} on k = 5 … 22; fitted on k ≤ 16 the offset model misses k = 22 by −4.3·10^{−4} and the stretched model by −7.5·10^{−4}, pure geometric by −1.5·10^{−3}.

6.3 A renormalisation reading and a prediction

Lift the level-k solution to level k + 1 (its top-digit sensitivity is then 0) and apply F once: the injected top-digit sensitivity is exactly (3/4)s_k at λ = 2, because the mean is preserved and only the auxiliary term, whose mass fraction is 3/4, selects lifts by the new digit. Iterating to the fixed point amplifies it by A_k = s_{k+1}/((3/4)s_k) = 1.20 → 1.23 for k = 8 … 21, and the per-level ratio of 1 − r_k is (3/4)A_k. Hence λ_k → 2 iff A∞ < 4/3. The amplification comes from the ×4 term recirculating top-digit variation with weight 1/4; if a fraction e of that variation survives the minimum, A = 1/(1 − e/4). Writing the fixed point as c = P + Q with P the ×4 term (mass fraction 0.275 → 0.262, tending to 1/4) and Q the auxiliary term, the surviving fraction is e_k = 0.614, 0.666, 0.692, 0.705, 0.715, 0.724, 0.731, 0.737, 0.742, 0.746 for k = 8 … 17, with shrinking increments; and the argmins of P and Q over the top digit agree on 0.332–0.333 of the classes at every k, exactly as if independent. If e∞ ≈ 0.78–0.80 the per-level ratio saturates near (3/4)/(1 − e∞/4) ≈ 0.935–0.94, and 1 − r_k eventually decays geometrically: λ_k → 2.

The prediction is therefore that the ratios keep rising but level off near 0.94 rather than approaching 1. k = 22 gives 0.924, still rising by about 0.002 per level, consistent and not decisive; at the current rate 0.94 would be reached near k = 31. What a proof would need is that the two argmins stay independent and the two variations stay comparable in the limit measure.

6.4 The limit object

The total variation between the level-j marginals of the normalised solutions at consecutive k decays geometrically (ratio ≈ 0.92), so a limit measure on Z₃ exists. It is singular: C_max/C_min grows like 1.52^k (147 at k = 12, 9851 at k = 22), the mass in the top 1 % of classes rises from 6.5 % to over 9 %, and the Hölder exponent of the digit profile on coarse scales falls from 0.14 (k = 12) to 0.085 (k = 19), extrapolating to 0.04–0.09, possibly 0. The boundary layer is universal: the Hölder exponent at distance d from the top digit is 0.665, 0.401, 0.293, 0.232, 0.194 for d = 1 … 5, identical for every k to three decimals, which looks like a fixed point of “append a digit, then minimise”. Integrating the limit inequality on X = {m ∈ Z₃ : m ≡ 2 (mod 3)} with Haar measure gives 1 ≤ λ^{−2} + (1/3)(λ^{α−2} + λ^{α−1}) with equality exactly at λ = 2, so λ = 2 is the critical value of the limit problem and the finite-k deficit is precisely 1 − ρ_k.

6.5 The spectrum of the linearisation

At the Perron vector the minimum in every row is attained by a fixed lift, so F is a sparse matrix J there, with J[i, 4i] = λ^{−2} and J[i, argmin lift] = λ^{α−j}. We computed its spectrum (dense to k = 9, Arnoldi top-40 to k = 15) at λ = λ_k and λ = 2.

The gap does not close: |λ₂| is pinned at 0.86–0.87 while a ring of eigenvalues fills in at that radius, the finite-k shadow of the edge of the essential spectrum. The second eigenvector is delocalised: its energy is 0.667 on every ternary digit (the value for a vector with no digit structure) and 0.71 on the top digit, while the Perron vector’s digit profile decays geometrically from 0.38 to 0.004; λ₂ is complex with a rotating argument. For k ≤ 9 the full spectrum is a disc of radius ≈ 0.8 containing 99 % of the eigenvalues, a thin ring at 0.865, and the isolated Perron root. Consequently the k-dependence of 1 − ρ_k, and the slowing of the power iteration at large k (effective contraction ≈ 0.97 at k = 22), are not set by the linear mixing rate; they come from the minimum, that is, from argmin switching as the iterate moves and from non-normal transients. The question λ_k → 2 is a question about the minimum structure, which is what §6.3 addresses, not about the spectrum of the linearisation. The structural reason is that the doubling branch m ↦ 4m is an isometry of Z₃ and mixes nothing: on Hölder-α functions the essential radius should be near 1/4 + (3/4)3^{−α}, which approaches 1 as α → 0, and the limit vector’s Hölder exponent is at most 0.09.

6.6 Calibration on 5x+1

The same construction applies to T(n) = n/2, (5n+1)/2: principal classes a ≡ 3 (mod 5) (odd predecessor (2a − 1)/5), four doublings return to a principal class (λ^{−4} c_{16a}), and the odd predecessor falls into one of five cases by a mod 25 (weight λ^{β−1−d} with β = log₂ 5 and d = 0, 1, 2, 3 further doublings, or dropped when it is ≡ 0 mod 5), with the minimum over five lifts. A residue-walking LP (HiGHS, constraints generated by following the map on residues, no index formulas) reproduces Table 2 for 3x+1 to 10^{−6} and matches the 5x+1 index formulas to 10^{−8} for k ≤ 6; a Metal kernel matches the NumPy map to 10^{−7}.

Integrating the 5x+1 limit inequality gives 1 ≤ λ^{−4} + (λ^β/5)(λ^{−1} + λ^{−2} + λ^{−3} + λ^{−4}), with equality again exactly at λ = 2: the same mass-conservation identity, although the predecessor exponent of 5x+1 is believed to be η₅ ≈ 0.65 (Kontorovich–Lagarias; Volkov 0.678). For 5x+1, then, the loss 1 − ρ_k(5) must not vanish if that belief is right; it is a case where λ∞ < 2 is the expected truth.

In both cases the lesson for 3x+1 is that a rising ratio is not by itself evidence for ρ_k → 1; only the mechanism can decide. (No explicit predecessor-count exponent for 5x+1 exists in the literature; π_a(x) ≥ x^{0.516} numerically at k = 14 appears to be the first, pending the same certification.)

7. What is and is not established

All results here are density statements, and even the limit γ → 1 would exclude neither a nontrivial cycle nor a divergent orbit. Cycles are the province of Diophantine approximation: with Barina’s verification to 2^{71} and Hercher’s analysis, a nontrivial cycle has at least 1.375·10^{11} odd terms and period at least 2.18·10^{11}. Divergent orbits have no tool at all; Tao’s theorem leaves them as a set of logarithmic density zero. Conway’s undecidability result for maps linear on residue classes implies that any proof must use the arithmetic of 2 and 3 specifically; the Krasikov–Lagarias system does, but only through the branching rule mod 3 and the size factors 2 and 3/2, which is why it yields exponents and not individual orbits.

Within the method, the open mathematical problem is λ_k → 2 ⇔ ρ_k → 1, which §6 reduces to the alignment of two argmins in the limit measure on Z₃. We regard the data to k = 22 as consistent with that limit and not as evidence for it.
