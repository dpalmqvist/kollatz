## KQ1. Lean 4 / Mathlib: what Collatz material exists; Erdős problem 1135; ProofAtlas and Mazur's "collatz predecessor 0.90"

### Takeaway
Mathlib itself contains no Collatz material; the only "canonical" Lean statements are in DeepMind's `formal-conjectures` (where the Collatz conjecture is filed as Erdős problem 1135, which is simply the Collatz conjecture, not a predecessor-density statement). ProofAtlas (proofatlas.ai) is a one-person, AI-agent-driven "AI-first formal mathematics" site run by Lech Mazur; its Collatz entries (predecessor exponent 0.90 at k = 18 via `native_decide`, positive lower density of predecessors, Tao's theorem, log-time descent) are Lean-checked but reviewed only by AI (Claude) reviewers, not by humans or journals.

## KQ2. GitHub msharpe248/collatz

### Takeaway
A one-author, MIT-licensed repo (created 2026-08-31, last push 2026-09-06, 1 star, 88 commits) that kernel-checks ("decide +kernel", no `native_decide`, no floats) Krasikov–Lagarias-type certificates at k = 12, 13, 14 (γ = 0.8476 / 0.8569 / 0.8676) with sharding, plus Lean "no-go" theorems and an odd-step-density statement about hypothetical divergent orbits; the README itself says the conjecture is open and that several results are conditional on stated hypotheses. ## KQ4. Large certificates in proof assistants: native_decide vs kernel vs external verified checkers; reported scaling

### Takeaway
Practice splits into three tiers: (i) kernel reduction (`decide +kernel`, sharded) — smallest trust base but ~hours per million rows and tens of GB (msharpe248: 29 h/37 GB at 1.6 M classes; Mian–Siddique: 46 CPU-h for the EMW search); (ii) `native_decide`/reflection — adds the Lean compiler to the trust base (one generated axiom per computation since Lean 4.29) but handles 10^8–10^9 elements in minutes to tens of minutes at ~8 B/element plus overheads (Mazur 18.5 min/10.6 GiB at 129 M; ours 2335 s at 387 M with a tail-recursive loop); (iii) external verified checkers (cake_lpr, Coq/ACL2 checkers; streaming LRAT import) — the only route demonstrated for terabyte-scale SAT proofs. A July 2026 kernel soundness bug (exploited in an AI-assisted Collatz "disproof") is a reminder that independent checkers and current toolchains matter.

### Cited Findings
- Lean docs on `native_decide`: "Unlike decide, this uses #eval to evaluate the decidability instance… it adds the entire lean compiler to the trusted part, and the axiom ofReduceBool will show up in #print axioms for theorems using this method or anything that transitively depends on them." — [Lean tactic docs mirror](https://florisvandoorn.com/LeanCourse24/docs/Lean/Parser/Tactic.html)
- Lean 4.29.0 release notes (#12217): "native computation (`native_decide`, `bv_decide`) is represented in the logic as one axiom per computation"; `#print axioms` shows the generated names (e.g. `…._native.bv_decide.…`) instead of `Lean.trustCompiler`. — [Lean v4.29.0 release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/releases/v4.29.0/)
- Mazur's k = 18 checks (129,140,163 classes): LP certificate `native_decide` 18.5 min / 10.6 GiB peak; adaptive-potential certificate 14.6 min / 9.3 GiB; hex payload 516,560,652 bytes in 16 chunks ≤ 64 MiB via `include_str`. Our replication on an M5/24 GB: potential 568 s, LP (our weights, γ = 9032/10000) 818 s. Our k = 19 (387,420,489 classes) with a tail-recursive `LoopCheck` replacing `List.range N`: potential 1819 s (peak ~7.7 GB), LP 2335 s (~2.7 GB RSS plus swap). ### Inferences
- For our k = 20–21 certificates (1.2–3.5 ×10^9 variables, 4.3–13 GB float32), `native_decide` with a streaming/tail-recursive checker is the only Lean route in reach (≈30 GiB at k = 20, ≈90 GiB at k = 21 by our model); kernel checking at these sizes is out of the question by three orders of magnitude (extrapolating msharpe248's 29 h per 1.6 M rows).
- The SAT community's answer to the same problem is an *external* verified checker over a streamed certificate (cake_lpr, lrat-catcher). An analogous "verified LP-certificate checker" (checker correctness proved once in Lean, run natively over a streamed chunked certificate, as PBLean/lrat-catcher do) would give the same trust base as `native_decide` but constant memory in N — a plausible reusable design for k = 21. No such LP-certificate checker currently exists; Mazur's `include_str` hex chunks keep the whole payload resident.
- The July 2026 episode makes a second, independent checker (nanoda, lean4lean, lean4checker) and a pinned current toolchain part of the expected trust story for any Collatz-adjacent Lean claim.

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head memory/time benchmark of `native_decide` versus cake_lpr/lrat-catcher for the same certificate; the lrat-catcher paper's S(4)/R(4,4) numbers were not extracted (full text not fetched).
- No published figures for `native_decide` on 10^9 elements other than our own and Mazur's unpublished measurements.

## KQ5. GPU-scale verification of the convergence range: Barina 2^71, follow-ups, BOINC-era context

### Takeaway
Barina's refereed 2025 paper establishes convergence for all n < 2^71 (milestone 15 Jan 2025) using thousands of CPU/GPU workers on Czech and EU supercomputers (12,395 CPU-years + 159 GPU-years); his project continues (2075·2^60 ≈ 2^71.02 verified, working toward 2076·2^60 as of Oct 2026, GitHub still active 2026-10-05). Earlier limits: Oliveira e Silva 20·2^58 (Jan 2009), Roosendaal 2^60 (2011), yoyo@home 87·2^60 (2017, each number checked twice), Barina 2^68 (2020). No other 2025–2026 distributed effort was found.

### Cited Findings
- Barina, "Improved verification limit for the convergence of the Collatz conjecture", J. ## KQ6. 2024–2026 preprints: LP, transfer operators, spectral methods, 3-adic/Syracuse random walks
