# Adjacent Collatz literature frames the k = 21 program

The refereed record for the predecessor exponent is still Krasikov–Lagarias's γ = 0.84 (k = 11, Acta Arith. 2003), nobody has taken up their §6 question λ_k → 2 in print, and every 2024–2026 "improvement" (0.8676 kernel-checked at k = 14, 0.90 via `native_decide` at k = 18, this project's 0.9203 at k = 21) lives in unrefereed, AI-assisted Lean repositories — so the program's competitive position is strong but its publication venue is, at present, the same self-published tier as its rivals. On the cycle side, Barina's refereed 2^71 verification (milestone 2025-01-15) makes Hercher's Corollary 29 unconditional: every nontrivial cycle has at least 137 528 045 312 odd members and C-period at least 355 504 839 929, while the m-cycle bound m ≥ 92 was proved with X0 = 695·2^60 ≈ 2^69.4 (not 2^68, not 3·2^69) and received a corrigendum in June 2026. The four long-shot strands are all dormant rather than dead: Terras's coefficient stopping time conjecture is open but Rozier–Terracol (Discrete Math. 2026) have already pushed the equality check to n ≤ 2.8·10^19, so a "new" computation there must start past that; the Belaga–Mignotte 3x+d catalogue (d < 20 000) has had only one independent recount (Morley 2013, OEIS); Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule's rewriting reformulation has zero termination-tool follow-ups and its three $500 conjectures remain open; and no explicit predecessor exponent for 5x+1 exists anywhere (Kontorovich–Lagarias leave c1 unspecified), which makes a 5x+1 run of this project's LP a genuinely new result. The formal-verification landscape splits into three trust tiers — kernel reduction (hours per million rows), `native_decide` (minutes per 10^8–10^9 rows, compiler in the trust base), and streamed external verified checkers (terabyte SAT proofs) — and a July 2026 Lean kernel soundness bug, exploited by an AI-assisted Collatz "disproof", makes independent checkers and pinned toolchains part of any credible claim. Finally, the notes correct several misstatements in circulation: Applegate–Lagarias's Conjecture A is positive density, not (4/3)^k tree growth (that is Conjectures B, C, C′); Simons–de Weger's refereed bound is m ≤ 68, with m ≤ 75 only in a 2010 web preprint; and the published Applegate–Lagarias growth constants are 1.302053 and 1.358386, not the 1.299 and 1.361 that Lagarias's own bibliography prints.

**Provenance legend used throughout.** Every number carries a tag: **[P]** primary-confirmed (read in the primary source, its publisher page, or a locally extracted PDF; Lagarias's annotated bibliographies are treated as authoritative primary for pre-2010 items), **[S]** secondary (seen only via a citing paper, abstract snippet, OEIS comment, Wikipedia, or a GitHub README), **[U]** unverified (could not be checked at all), **[I]** internal measurement from this project's own runs (`/Users/daniel/devel/kollatz/CLAUDE.md` and `lean_formal/logs/axiom_audits.txt`). One tag at the end of a clause covers all numbers in that clause.

## Barina's 2^71 makes Hercher's Corollary 29 unconditional

The modern cycle-length argument is Eliahou's: the ratio (K+L)/K of a nontrivial T-cycle must be an upper semiconvergent of log_2 3, and the verification range X0 selects which numerators are admissible. Eliahou, "The 3x+1 problem: new lower bounds on nontrivial cycle lengths", Discrete Math. 118 (1993) 45–56, DOI 10.1016/0012-365X(93)90052-U, proves that any nontrivial T-cycle has period **p = 301994a + 17087915b + 85137581c** (a, b, c ≥ 0, b ≥ 1, ac = 0), hence **period ≥ 17 087 915**, using X0 = 2^40 ([Lagarias bib. I, entry 53](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224)) **[S, authoritative — the Elsevier full text returned HTTP 403; the abstract is confirmed at the publisher record](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:12087) [P]**. Halbeisen–Hungerbühler, "Optimal bounds for the length of rational Collatz cycles", Acta Arith. 78 (1997) 227–239, restate the criterion as k(2^40) = 17 087 915, note that Crandall's cruder estimate with the same 2^40 gave only 301 994, and show that verification to 212 366 032 807 211 ≈ 2.1·10^14 lifts the bound to **102 225 496**, which their note in proof records as met by Oliveira e Silva's 3·2^50 ([H–H PDF](https://math.ch/norbert.hungerbuehler/publications/Optimal_bounds_for_the_length_of_rational_Collatz_cycles.pdf)) **[P]**. The three Eliahou numerators are the convergent numerators p_13, p_15, p_16 of log_2 3 ([OEIS A005663](https://oeis.org/A005663)) **[P]**. Later re-applications: Sinisalo's Oulu preprint gives period ≥ 630 138 897 (= p_19) from Oliveira e Silva's 2.70·10^16 ([Lagarias bib. II, entry 105](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608208)) **[S]**; Lagarias's overview chapter (The Ultimate Challenge, AMS 2010; arXiv 2021) records as "W2" that the trivial cycle is the only one with period < 10 439 860 591 (= p_21) or with fewer than 6 586 818 670 (= q_21) odd members, from X0 = 20·2^58 ([arXiv:2111.02635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02635)) **[P]**. Note the indexing trap: Halbeisen–Hungerbühler number the convergents differently (their p_16 = 17 087 915, p_18 = 102 225 496 = p_16 + p_15 in the OEIS indexing) **[P]**.

The m-cycle line (m = number of local minima) is Simons–de Weger's. The refereed paper, "Theoretical and computational bounds for m-cycles of the 3n+1-problem", Acta Arith. 117 (2005) 51–70, DOI 10.4064/aa117-1-3, excludes **1 ≤ m ≤ 68** using Roosendaal's X0 ≈ 3.39·10^17 (Nov 2004) ([IMPAN](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/117/1/82263/theoretical-and-computational-bounds-for-m-cycles-of-the-3n-1-problem)) **[P; the exact multiplier 301 250·2^50 is inferred, the exponent was lost in PDF extraction — U]**. The authors' web version v1.41 reached m ≤ 74 ([Lagarias bib. II, entry 101](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608208)) **[S]**, and v1.44 (31 Aug 2010) states "there do not exist nontrivial m-cycles for 1 ≤ m ≤ 75" using Oliveira e Silva's X0 = 5·2^60 > 5.7646·10^18, with the residual cases m = 76, 77 pinned to explicit (K, L, x_min) rows and the general bounds 7.5311·10^11 < K < 1.4784·m·δ^m for 91 ≤ m ≤ 515 619 ([de Weger PDF v1.44](https://deweger.net/papers/%5B35a%5DSidW-3n+1-v1.44%5B2010%5D.pdf)) **[P]**. The transcendence input there is still Rhin's 1987 bound specific to x log 2 + y log 3, used as Λ > e^{−13.3(0.46057 + log K)} (Lemma 12), which Simons–de Weger judge better than Laurent–Mignotte–Nesterenko and Matveev for this case ([same PDF](https://deweger.net/papers/%5B35a%5DSidW-3n+1-v1.44%5B2010%5D.pdf)) **[P]**. No 2020–2026 paper sharpening the two-logarithm constant for |2^n − 3^k| and applying it to Collatz cycles was found; Gouillon's 2006 JTNB bound (constant ≈ 5·10^4 instead of ≈ 10^8) exists but is unapplied ([JTNB](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.537)) **[S]**. Hercher's 2023 paper imports its K-upper bound from Simons–de Weger and contains no transcendence step of its own ([Hercher JIS](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL26/Hercher/hercher5.html)) **[P]**, so an improved two-log constant would enter only through Lemma 12 and raise the excluded m only logarithmically.

Hercher, "There are no Collatz m-cycles with m ≤ 91", Journal of Integer Sequences 26 (2023), Article 23.3.5, 22 pp. (arXiv:2201.00406, v1 2 Jan 2022, v3 4 Apr 2023; Zbl 1536.11052, reviewer O. Rozier), defines X0 as the largest verified range and states **"As of the date of writing this paper, we have X0 = 695·2^60"**, with the proof of Theorem 23 using "X0 = 695·2^60 ≈ 8·10^20" ([JIS article](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL26/Hercher/hercher5.html), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00406)) **[P]**; the ar5iv rendering of the arXiv text instead says 704·2^60 ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.00406)) **[P]**. Both are ≈ 2^69.4. The number 3·2^69 = 1536·2^60 appears only in Corollary 29: if X0 ≥ 3·2^69 then every nontrivial cycle has **K > 1.375·10^11** odd members — proved by a C++ program over residue classes mod powers of two, dropping cases whose smallest residue member exceeds 3781·2^60, in "five weeks of computing time on an i9 processor of the 11th generation with 8 cores" **[P]**. Remark 28 explains the mechanism: the Eliahou-type argument needs X0 ≥ 3781·2^60 to reach that threshold; Hercher's Theorem 27 (Σ T(n_i) < K·(3/4)/X0, independent of m) lowers it to 2836·2^60, and the residue case analysis to 1536·2^60, "nearly 60%" less than the trivial requirement **[P]**. The main-theorem iteration starts from Simons–de Weger's K > 7·10^11 (Hercher's rounding of their 7.5311·10^11 **[P]**) and climbs through K > 5.2·10^15, 3.97·10^17, 4.64·10^18, 2.74·10^19, 7.76·10^19, 2.05·10^20 to a contradiction with K < 1.4784·m·δ^m < 2.2·10^20 at m2 ≥ 91 **[P]**. The version history matters for citation: arXiv v1 and v2 claimed m ≥ 91 (title "m ≤ 90"), v3 and the JIS version m ≥ 92 **[P for the listing]**. A **corrigendum dated 14 June 2026** repairs the derivation of the inequalities T(n_{m−m2+1+ℓ}) < 3/(2^v−1)^δ at the bottom of page 14 in the proof of Theorem 21 by a convexity argument; the m ≤ 91 result stands ([corrigendum PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL26/Hercher/corrigendum.pdf)) **[P]**; Rozier's zbMATH review had already flagged "a potential flaw near the end" of that proof **[P]**. Hercher's earlier Die Wurzel articles (6/2018 and 7/2018) verified all starts ≤ 87·2^60 ≈ 1.003·10^20 and proved a C-period > 17 026 679 261 (= p_21 + q_21) ([Flensburg PDF](https://www.uni-flensburg.de/fileadmin/content/abteilungen/mathematik/hercher/forschung/wurzel-artikel-collatz.pdf)) **[P]**; the K > 7.2·10^10 bound credited to Hercher and Puchert (Die Wurzel 11 (2018) 243–250) corresponds to excluding the semiconvergent pair (114 208 327 604, 72 057 431 991) **[S; Puchert's text unverified — U]**.

Barina, "Improved verification limit for the convergence of the Collatz conjecture", The Journal of Supercomputing 81, art. 810 (2025), DOI 10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0 (accepted 21 April 2025), verifies convergence for **all n < 2^71 = 2048·2^60**, and its §6 says explicitly that this "is the moment when the length of a non-trivial cycle rises to 355 504 839 929 [Hercher 2023]"; Table 10 dates the milestones 2^68 (2020-05-07), 2^69 (2021-12-10), 2^70 (2023-07-09), **2^71 (2025-01-15)** ([VUT record](https://www.fit.vut.cz/research/result/c197809/.en), [postprint PDF](https://www.fit.vut.cz/research/publication-file/c197809/298514/s11227-025-07337-0.pdf)) **[P]**. The run consumed 12 395 CPU-years and 159 GPU-years on MetaCentrum, IT4Innovations (Karolina) and LUMI, with a 1 335× aggregate acceleration and the best GPU implementation 52× faster than the best CPU one; the abstract says "four new path records" while §6 lists five (OEIS A006884) — an internal inconsistency **[P]**. The live project page (generated 2026-10-08) shows **2075·2^60 ≈ 2^71.02** verified, working toward 2076·2^60, with no later milestone ([pcbarina.fit.vutbr.cz](https://pcbarina.fit.vutbr.cz/)) **[P]**; the code repository (MIT, 84 stars, 1 521 commits) was last pushed 2026-10-05 ([xbarin02/collatz](https://github.com/xbarin02/collatz)) **[P]**. Since 2048·2^60 > 1536·2^60, Corollary 29 holds unconditionally from 2025-01-15: **K ≥ q_23 = 137 528 045 312, T-period ≥ p_23 = 217 976 794 617, C-period ≥ 355 504 839 929** (arithmetic checked locally against OEIS A005663 and Wikipedia) **[P for the inputs; the composition is the notes' own inference]**. Two caveats shape what "current state" means. First, nobody has published a re-run of Hercher's Theorem 23 iteration with X0 = 2^71, so the refereed m-bound is still m ≥ 92 at X0 = 695·2^60; the only "m ≥ 102" claim is a single-commit GitHub Lean repo (justinalick, 2026, "not yet refereed", prepared with Claude Code) that assumes Barina's 2^71 and a "RhinBound" as hypotheses ([repo](https://github.com/justinalick/collatz-m-cycles-101-lean)) **[U]**. Second, no published figure says what X0 would exclude the next pair (p_23, q_23) and force K ≥ q_24 = 753 110 839 881; the project's own estimate of roughly 2^75 is unpublished **[I]**. Angeltveit's "An improved algorithm for checking the Collatz conjecture for all n < 2^N" (arXiv:2602.10466, 11 Feb 2026) claims that verifying n < 2^{N+1} costs less than twice the work of n < 2^N and cites 2^71 as state of the art, but reports no run ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10466)) **[P for abstract; the "2^77 reachable / 2^75 modest goal" sentences are from search snippets — S]**. Bouhamidi's arXiv:2601.17573 (Jan 2026, math.GM) applies its own theorems with M0 = 2^71 and recovers only L ≥ 217 976 794 617, nothing beyond Hercher ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17573)) **[P]**.

## The refereed exponent record is still 0.84 and nobody has touched λ_k → 2

The refereed chain of exponents in π_a(x) ≥ x^γ for a ≢ 0 (mod 3) runs: Crandall, Math. Comp. 32 (1978) 1281–1292, proves some γ > 0 exists and "his proof seems to give c = 0.05" ([Lagarias bib. I](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224)) **[S]**; Sander, Acta Arith. 55 (1990) 241–248, γ = 3/10 by extending Crandall **[S]**; Krasikov, "How many numbers satisfy the 3x+1 Conjecture?", Internat. J. Math. & Math. Sci. 12 (1989) 791–796, introduces the mod-3^k difference inequalities and gets **γ = 3/7** at k = 2 ([EMIS PDF](https://emis.univie.ac.at/journals/HOA/IJMMS/Volume12_4/362647.pdf)) **[S for the exponent; P for the PDF's existence]**; Wirsching, Acta Arith. 63 (1993) 205–210, γ = 0.48 at k = 3 **[S]**. Applegate–Lagarias, "Density bounds for the 3x+1 problem I. Tree-search method", Math. Comp. 64 (1995) 411–426, prove Theorem 1.2 verbatim as "π_a(x) ≥ c_a x^{.65} for all x ≥ a" with the optimised exponent **γ*_30 = .654717** from a depth-30 search ([AL-I PDF](https://websites.umich.edu/~lagarias/doc/applegateI.pdf), [AMS](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1995-64-209/S0025-5718-1995-1270612-0/)) **[P]**, which Lagarias's bibliography rounds to x^{.643} **[P]**. Part II, "Krasikov inequalities", Math. Comp. 64 (1995) 427–438, gives **π_a(x) > x^{.809}** from a nonlinear program with about 20 000 variables ([bib. I entry 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224), [PDF](https://dept.math.lsa.umich.edu/~lagarias/doc/applegateII.pdf)) **[S via bibliography]**; the AMS abstract page prints 0.81 in one place and .804 in another, a version or rounding discrepancy ([AMS](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1995-64-209/S0025-5718-1995-1270613-2/)) **[P]**. Krasikov–Lagarias, "Bounds for the 3x+1 problem using difference inequalities", Acta Arith. 109 (2003) 237–258, arXiv:math/0205002 (single version, 30 Apr 2002), announce a "computer-aided proof that at least x^{0.84}" of integers below x reach 1; the abstract does not mention k = 11, and the bibliography states the result as x^{.841} from the k = 11 program ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205002), [IMPAN](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/109/3/81918/bounds-for-the-3x-1-problem-using-difference-inequalities), [bib. II](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608208)) **[P]**. Lagarias's overview still lists "(W5) ... at least X^{0.84}" and closes with: "It remains an open problem to show that for each ε > 0 there exists a positive constant c(ε) such that π_1(x) > c(ε)x^{1−ε}" ([arXiv:2111.02635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02635)) **[P]**. Two structural facts follow. Every refereed exponent ≥ 0.65 is a computer-aided proof (AL-I calls its proof "unavoidably computer-intensive"), and none was formally verified at publication **[P]**. And the methods split: Crandall/Sander count the preimage tree directly, while Krasikov/Wirsching/AL-II/KL use the difference inequalities; AL-I concedes on p. 4 that "Krasikov's inequalities give still better exponents" **[P]**.

The Applegate–Lagarias conjectures are frequently misquoted, so the exact statements matter. **Conjecture A** (AL-I §1, verbatim): "For each a ≢ 0 (mod 3), there is a positive constant c_a such that π_a(x) ≥ c_a x for all x ≥ a" — positive density, preceded by the remark that for a ≡ 0 (mod 3) the preimages are exactly {2^k a} ([AL-I PDF](https://websites.umich.edu/~lagarias/doc/applegateI.pdf)) **[P]**. **Conjecture B** (attributed to Lagarias–Weiss, Ann. Appl. Probab. 2 (1992) 229–261): "For each a ≢ 0 (mod 3), n_k(a) = (4/3)^{k(1+o(1))} as k → ∞", where Lagarias–Weiss had proved only the average over a of n_k(a) grows like (4/3)^k and the a priori bounds (1/2)(√2)^k ≤ n_k(a) ≤ 2(√3)^k **[P]**. **Conjecture C**: "Both N^+(k) and N^−(k) are (4/3)^{k(1+o(1))}" for the extremal pruned-tree leaf counts, "stronger than Conjecture B, because it concerns extreme values over all trees of depth k" **[P]**. **Conjecture C′**: "There are positive constants C^+ and C^− such that C^−(4/3)^k ≤ N^−(k) < N^+(k) ≤ C^+(4/3)^k for all sufficiently large k" — and the authors note that for the branching-process models B[3^j], j ≥ 2, the analogue of C is true but C′ is false, so 3x+1 trees have narrower leaf-count variation than random models, "the first significant deviation found for the 3x+1 iteration from being as random as possible" **[P]**. Theorem 1.1 prints **(1.302053)^k ≤ n_k(a) ≤ (1.358386)^k** for large k; the bibliography quotes 1.299 and 1.361, and the paper's numbers should be preferred **[P for both; reason for the discrepancy undetermined]**. The extremal vectors were computed for all 1 ≤ k ≤ 30, "memory was exhausted by the hashtable at k = 21", the number of distinct depth-k shapes grows like θ^k with 1.87 < θ < 1.92, and the authors conjecture γ*_k → 1 **[P]**. No later work extends the extremal computation past depth 30; the CLAUDE.md remark that Applegate–Lagarias searched to depth 60 refers to their total-stopping-time paper (arXiv:math/0103054), not to leaf counts **[I]**. Kontorovich–Lagarias (arXiv:0910.1944, AMS 2010) prove in Theorem 6.1 that for a uniformly random residue class a mod 3^{k+1}, a ≢ 0 (mod 3), the expected number of leaves of the pruned depth-k tree is exactly (4/3)^k, and name the "tree growth constant" conjecture δ_3(a) = log(4/3) ([arXiv:0910.1944](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1944)) **[P]**; this, not Conjecture A, is the "3x+1 Growth Exponent Conjecture" that the neljaouh repository claims as a corollary.

On the open problem itself: Krasikov–Lagarias §6 observe λ_k ≤ λ_{k+1}, pose strict increase as open, and note that π_a(x) ≥ x^{1−ε} for all ε follows if λ_k → 2 **[I, from the project's reading of the paper]**. The notes found no refereed or preprint progress. Lagarias's bibliography II describes "the (not yet realized) hope of proving π_a(x) > x^{1−ε} by this approach, taking a sufficiently large k" ([arXiv:math/0608208](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608208)) **[P]**; a search summary of the 2021 overview says the record "may have an underlying structure involving an infinite family of nonlinear programs of increasing complexity" **[S, wording unverified]**; and Tavares (2026) characterises the KL bound as "a sublinear count with an a-dependent threshold" weaker than the uniform positive density Wirsching's chain targets ([arXiv:2608.27617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27617)) **[P]**. The project's own transfer-operator formulation on Z_3 (λ = 2 ⇔ equality a.e., ρ_k → 1) has no competitor in the literature; searches for "transfer operator Collatz", "Collatz Perron" and "Krasikov inequalities" in 2024–2026 returned nothing applied to the K–L system, the nearest items being Mori's operator-theoretic arXiv:2411.08084 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08084)) **[P, listing only]** and Hateley's preprints.org manuscript on a backward transfer operator with spectral radius 1 and a positive invariant density in a dyadic–triadic multiscale space ([preprints.org 202511.1440](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202511.1440/v6/download)) **[S, not read in full]**.

One level up sits Wirsching's positive-density programme: Wirsching, *The Dynamical System Generated by the 3n+1 Function*, LNM 1681, Springer 1998 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0095985)) **[P]**, and "On the problem of positive predecessor density in 3N+1 dynamics", Discrete Contin. Dyn. Syst. 9 (2003) 771–787, which "formulates three conjectures which, if proved, would establish" positive density for every a ≢ 0 (mod 3) ([bib. II](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608208), [preprint](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/baake/algdyn/posden.pdf)) **[P]**. Tavares, "Wirsching's Positive-Predecessor-Density Program: Proofs of Conjectures 1 and 3", arXiv:2608.27617 (math.GM, 27 Aug 2026, 36 pp.), claims Conjectures 1 (Theorem 4.3) and 3 and states that Conjecture 2 "remains open" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27617)) **[P; proofs not checked]**; math.GM plus no journal reference means it is a claim pending review. Tao's theorem — "Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values", Forum Math. Pi 10 (2022) e12, arXiv:1909.03562 — is a logarithmic-density statement ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562)) **[P]**; one fetch of the abstract page listed versions v6 (6 Jul 2026) and v7 (16 Jul 2026) while a cached copy stopped at v5 **[U, check the live page]**. The only natural-density strengthening found is Mazur's ProofAtlas item "Natural-density Collatz descent in logarithmic time" (density-one descent below the start within 436·log N raw steps, "Accepted · Lean checked, 4 reviews") ([ProofAtlas advances](https://www.proofatlas.ai/advances/)) **[P for the page; U for the mathematics]** — a different statement from Tao's (weaker in threshold, stronger in density), not a strict improvement. Korec's older natural-density result (some iterate below n^β for density one when β > log 3/log 4 ≈ 0.7925; corrected 2026-10-10, AL-I p. 4 prints β_c := log 3/log 4) is the refereed comparison point ([AL-I PDF](https://websites.umich.edu/~lagarias/doc/applegateI.pdf)) **[P]**.

## Rozier–Terracol already moved Terras's equality check to 2.8·10^19

**Strand A, Terras.** Terras, "A stopping time problem on the positive integers", Acta Arith. 30 (1976) 241–252, defines the coefficient stopping time κ(n) (least k with 3^{a(n)}/2^k < 1 in T^{(k)}(n) = α(n)n + β(n)), observes κ(n) ≤ σ(n), conjectures κ(n) = σ(n) for all n ≥ 2, and proves it for all n with **κ(n) ≤ 2593** ([Lagarias bib. I, entry 164](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224)) **[P]**. Garner, "On the Collatz 3n+1 algorithm", Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 82 (1981) 19–22, extends this to **κ(n) < 105 000**, using the continued fraction of log_2 3 and the conjecture verified to n < 2.0·10^9 ([bib. I, entry 71](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224)) **[P]**. Lagarias's 1985 Monthly survey (92, 3–23) states the conjecture in Section 2 and notes it implies the absence of nontrivial cycles ([HTML edition](https://www.cecm.sfu.ca/organics/papers/lagarias/paper/html/node4.html)) **[P; the numeric CST bound there is lost in the HTML conversion — U]**. The decisive modern item is Rozier–Terracol, "Paradoxical behavior in Collatz sequences", arXiv:2502.00948 (v1 2 Feb 2025, v5 17 May 2026), **Discrete Mathematics 349 (2026) 115167, DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2026.115167**: τ(n) is exactly Terras's κ; Theorem 1.3 finds exactly **593 paradoxical sequences** with first term ≤ 4614 (550 distinct starts, exhaustive to 10^9); Corollary 3.3 says finitely many paradoxical sequences imply Collatz; Theorem 5.3 uses the Oliveira e Silva / Roosendaal excursion and delay record tables to exclude further paradoxical sequences with 93 ≤ j ≤ 301 993 or 4615 ≤ n ≤ 2.8·10^19; and **Corollary 5.4 gives t(n) = τ(n) for every 2 ≤ n ≤ 2.8·10^19** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00948), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00948)) **[P]**. So the computational frontier of this strand has already moved from ~10^5 (in κ) to ~10^19 (in n) in 2025–26, resting on record tables rather than an independent exhaustive check; a new run would have to go past 2.8·10^19 — Barina's 2^71 ≈ 2.4·10^21 is the natural next input, and the Terras/Garner argument scales with both the verified range and the convergents **[inference]**. An unresolved conflict: Rozier–Terracol's text says the conjecture was "verified up to n = 250,000 (Terras) and extended to n = 1,150,000 (Garner)", whereas Lagarias gives bounds in κ; Garner's PAMS page returned 403, so the 250 000 / 1 150 000 figures are **[U]**. Winkler's arXiv:1709.03385 (v8 Jul 2026, math.GM) studies κ via parity vectors but, by its own abstract, proves neither finiteness nor equality ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03385)) **[P]**.

**Strand B, 3x+d cycles.** Lagarias, "The set of rational cycles for the 3x+1 problem", Acta Arith. 56 (1990) 33–53, DOI 10.4064/aa-56-1-33-53, studies integer cycles of T_k(x) = (3x+k)/2, x/2 for k ≡ ±1 (mod 6) with (x, k) = 1 (equivalently rational cycles x/k of T), conjectures every T_k has such a cycle, shows infinitely many k have ≥ k^{1−ε} cycles of period ≤ log k and infinitely many have none of period < k^{1/3}, and bounds C(k, 1.01k) ≤ 5k(log k)^5 ([bib. I, entry 108](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309224), [IMPAN](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/56/1/106574/the-set-of-rational-cycles-for-the-3x-1-problem)) **[P]**; the finiteness conjecture (finitely many primitive cycles per k) is confirmed only secondhand via Morley's OEIS comment ([A226607](https://oeis.org/A226607)) **[S]**, and OEIS A102280 reports two errors in the paper's Table 3.1 (k = 35 and k = 73) **[S]**. Belaga–Mignotte, "Embedding the 3x+1 Conjecture in a 3x+d Context", Experimental Math. 7 (1998) 145–151, prove at most d·k^c periodic orbits with ≤ k odd members (c effectively computable, via Baker–Wüstholz) ([Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/experimental-mathematics/volume-7/issue-2/Embedding-the-3x1-conjecture-in-a-3xd-context/em/1048515662.full)) **[P]**; their Strasbourg preprint 2000-18 (57 pp., HAL hal-00129656) computes "complete, as we argue" primitive-cycle lists for **all 6 667 systems with 1 ≤ d ≤ 19 999** ([HAL API](https://api.archives-ouvertes.fr/search/?q=halId_s:hal-00129656&fl=title_s,abstract_s,authFullName_s,producedDate_s)) **[P]**; Belaga, Acta Arith. 106 (2003) 197–206, gives prg(C) ≤ d/(2^{l/k} − 3) and U_{d,k} ≤ d·k^{32} ([bib. II, entry 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608208)) **[P]**; the 2006 data tables (preprint 2006-015, "9 pages + 400+ page table", hal-00129727) and the companion factorisation table of 2^l − 3^k for l ≤ 114 are on HAL ([HAL API](https://api.archives-ouvertes.fr/search/?q=halId_s:hal-00129727&fl=title_s,abstract_s,authFullName_s,producedDate_s)) **[P]**; "Walking Cautiously Into the Collatz Wilderness", DMTCS Proc. AG (2005) 249–260, DOI 10.46298/dmtcs.3512 ([DMTCS](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/3512)) **[P]**. The only later independent check is Morley's OEIS work (13 Jun 2013): for k < 20 000 he found **42 757 primitive cycles** by examining every trajectory starting below 10^8, largest minimal element 8 013 899 at k = 17 021, and found Belaga–Mignotte's total of 42 765 to be **8 too high** (a table-counting error, not missing cycles); a partial sweep to k < 158 195 found no primitive cycle with minimal element > 28 306 063 ([A226607](https://oeis.org/A226607), [A226613](https://oeis.org/A226613)) **[P]**. Cox, JIS 15 (2012) 12.5.2, fits a Poisson(1) model to cycles per d and recomputes to d = 10 000 over positive and negative n ([JIS PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL15/Cox/cox10.pdf)) **[P]**; Cox–Ghosh–Sultanow, J. Math. Computer Sci. 24 (2022) 330–337, arXiv:2101.04067, extend the Halbeisen–Hungerbühler bounds to 3n+c ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04067)) **[P, abstract]**. No 2014–2026 extension beyond d < 20 000 (complete) or d < 158 195 (partial) was found, so a GPU sweep to d ~ 10^6 with starts ≤ 2^40 would be new data for both the Poisson heuristic and Lagarias's existence/finiteness conjectures **[inference]**. Relevant internal fact: the project's `kl_d.py` found the K–L LP for 3x+d isomorphic to d = 1 (λ_k(d) = λ_k(1), verified k ≤ 7), so 3x+d offers cycle data but no new density exponent **[I]**.

**Strand C, rewriting termination.** Yolcu, Aaronson, Heule, "An Automated Approach to the Collatz Conjecture", arXiv:2105.14697 (v1 31 May 2021, v3 31 Dec 2022); CADE-28, LNCS 12699 (2021), DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-79876-5_27; J. Automated Reasoning 67, art. 15 (2023), DOI 10.1007/s10817-022-09658-8 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14697), [JAR](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10817-022-09658-8), [full PDF](https://emreyolcu.com/research/rewriting-collatz.pdf)) **[P]**. The reformulation is an **11-rule** string rewriting system T over {f, t, 0, 1, 2, ◁, ▷} (binary digits f/t, ternary digits 0/1/2, mixed-base representation): dynamic rules f▷ → ▷, t▷ → 2▷; auxiliary rules f0 → 0f, f1 → 0t, f2 → 1f, t0 → 1t, t1 → 2f, t2 → 2t, ◁0 → ◁t, ◁1 → ◁ff, ◁2 → ◁ft; **Theorem 3.17: T is terminating iff the Collatz conjecture holds** **[P]**. Theorem 3.8 proves Zantema's unary system Z admits no natural-matrix-interpretation termination proof of any dimension, extended to dependency pairs in Theorem 3.10 **[P]**. Automated successes: W′ (2-dimensional interpretation "in less than a second"), Farkas' variant, **all 11 single-rule-deleted subsystems of T** (dimensions up to 5, 30 s timeout), Theorem 4.9 (a nonconvergent trajectory cannot avoid any residue class mod 4) and Theorem 4.10 (cannot avoid 2, 3, 4, 6 mod 8); open with a **$500 reward each**: Conjectures 4.11–4.13 for the classes 1, 5, 7 mod 8; also open W′′ and the busy-beaver system BB **[P]**. Tooling: their own SAT-based prover using CaDiCaL ([emreyolcu/rewriting-collatz](https://github.com/emreyolcu/rewriting-collatz), 13 stars, 7 commits) **[P]**. The Semantic Scholar citation sweep found 15 citing papers, none on rewriting or matrix interpretations ([S2 API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2105.14697/citations?fields=title,year,authors,venue,externalIds&limit=200)) **[S]**; no evidence that T or its subsystems entered TPDB or the Termination Competition ([termination-portal.org](https://termination-portal.org/wiki/Termination_Competition)) **[S]**; the only 2026 relative is Carelli, "Loop Termination and Generalized Collatz Sequences", arXiv:2605.15094, accepted to ICALP 2026 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15094)) **[P, abstract]**. Whether the $500 conjectures have been claimed anywhere could not be determined **[U]**. The strand is dormant, not exhausted; a modern push means larger matrix dimensions and the untried interpretation classes the authors list, with the expected outcome — as the project's own note anticipates — negative results rather than a proof.

**Strand D, 5x+1.** Kontorovich–Lagarias, "Stochastic Models for the 3x+1 and 5x+1 Problems", arXiv:0910.1944, in *The Ultimate Challenge* (AMS 2010) 131–188, give the only published statement: Theorem 7.6, "There is a positive constant c1 such that" π_{a,5}(x) ≥ x^{c1} for a ≢ 0 (mod 5), x ≥ x0(a), proved by remarking that the Applegate–Lagarias inverse-tree methods "carry over", with the explicit admission "we suspect it is well below either of the above predictions" ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0910.1944)) **[P]**. Conjecture 7.2 asserts the growth exponent η5 exists, is a-independent and satisfies η5 < 1; the repeated random walk and a branching random walk both suggest **η5 ≈ 0.649**, Volkov's model ≈ 0.678, and the overview elsewhere writes 0.65049 and 0.65041 (5th-digit inconsistency) **[P]**; the branching-process value "η_{5,BP} ≈ 0.650919" is from a search snippet **[S]**. Volkov, Stoch. Proc. Appl. 116 (2006) 662–674 ([IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/spapps/v116y2006i4p662-674.html)) **[S, paywalled]**. Oliveira e Silva's page (28 Dec 2015) verifies a 5x+1 variant (dividing by 2 or 3 when possible) to ≈ 1.0·10^16 but reports no count of starts reaching 1 ([sweet.ua.pt](https://sweet.ua.pt/tos/px+1.html)) **[P]**. Nothing in Wirsching's book or Lagarias's bibliographies treats px+1 predecessor trees quantitatively **[P, negative]**. The conclusion is sharp: the 5x+1 inverse tree is structurally the K–L setup with residues mod 5^k, so this project's LP machinery would produce the first explicit c1; a bound above 0.651 would even discriminate between the Kontorovich–Lagarias and Volkov predictions **[inference]**. Two one-line references requested: Chamberland, "A Continuous Extension of the 3x+1 Problem to the Real Line", Dyn. Contin. Discrete Impuls. Syst. 2 (1996) 495–509 **[P via bib. I entry 35]**; Bernstein–Lagarias, "The 3x+1 Conjugacy Map", Canad. J. Math. 48 (1996) 1154–1169 (Φ: Z_2 → Z_2 conjugating the shift to T, odd fixed points −1 and 1/3) **[P via bib. I entry 23]**.

## Three trust tiers: kernel at 10^6 rows, native_decide at 10^9, streamed checkers at terabytes

Mathlib contains no Collatz material (a `gh` code search over leanprover-community/mathlib4 on 2026-10-08 returns no files), and the only canonical statement is DeepMind's formal-conjectures entry filing the Collatz conjecture itself as Erdős problem 1135 — a `sorry`-stubbed `erdos_1135`, with the erdosproblems.com page noting it is "not a problem due to Erdős" and that the $500 figure was never an offered prize ([erdosproblems.com/1135](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1135), [1135.lean](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1135.lean)) **[P]**. Mazur's `Erdos1135` namespace is workspace bookkeeping, not an Erdős problem about density **[P]**.

The strongest competing claims all sit on ProofAtlas, a one-person "AI-first formal mathematics" site run by Lech Mazur, whose entries are Lean-checked and reviewed only by AI ([proofatlas.ai](https://proofatlas.ai/)) **[P]**. The 0.90 artifact (published 17 Jul 2026, source commit 5f76a170…, 58 Lean files, 10 237 lines) proves `predecessor_count_lower_bound_090_nat`: for every target ≢ 0 mod 3, eventually cutoff^{9/10} ≤ predecessorCount; its axioms are the standard three plus two `native_decide` axioms (`k18AdaptiveForcedPotentialCertificate_check` and `k18Gamma901EncodedCertificate_check`); the certificates are a level-18 LP with **129 140 163 rows** and an adaptive-potential system with **215 233 605 inequalities**; "Four selected reviews, all by Claude Fable 5, with independence not asserted"; no explicit cutoff, no optimality claim ([artifact page](https://www.proofatlas.ai/proofs/artifact.collatz.predecessor-lower-bound-090.nat.v001.html), [source](https://proofatlas.ai/collatz-predecessor-090/)) **[P]**. Mazur's own cost figures are LP check 18.5 min / 10.6 GiB and potential check 14.6 min / 9.3 GiB at k = 18, with a 516 560 652-byte hex payload in 16 chunks ≤ 64 MiB via `include_str` **[I, from the 570 MB tarball]**. The "Positive Lower Density of Collatz Predecessors" formalization (manuscript v1.0, 6 Sep 2026; theorem `predecessors_positive_lower_density`; 388 files / 61 800 lines; Lean v4.30.0-rc2; OpenAI Codex credited; axioms only propext/Classical.choice/Quot.sound, "no generated native-evaluation axiom"; four AI reviews, "no independent external review is claimed") gives c(a) > 0 and X0(a) with at least c(a)·X integers below X reaching a — and its page describes no proof method ([ProofAtlas](https://www.proofatlas.ai/formalizations/positive-lower-density-collatz-predecessors/)) **[P for the page; U for the mathematics]**. ProofAtlas also lists a Tao formalization (397 files / 124 019 lines, axioms not displayed) and two further Collatz "advances" (density-one descent within 436·log N steps; cX integers reaching 1 within 10.46 ln n steps, review open) ([formalizations](https://www.proofatlas.ai/formalizations/), [advances](https://www.proofatlas.ai/advances/)) **[P]**. Context on credibility: Terence Tao's 12 Aug 2026 digestion of Mazur's Sendov proof and his `teorth/sendov` re-formalization (80 files, no `native_decide`) state that priority "belongs there" ([reservoir teorth/sendov](https://reservoir.lean-lang.org/@teorth/sendov)) **[P]** — strong evidence that Mazur's Lean pipeline works, and no evidence at all about his Collatz density argument, which no human expert has digested **[P, negative]**.

Three GitHub repositories complete the picture. msharpe248/collatz (MIT, created 2026-08-31, last push 2026-09-06, 88 commits, 1 star) kernel-checks K–L-type certificates with `decide +kernel`, no `native_decide`, no floats: **k = 14, π_1(x) = Ω(x^{0.8676})**, 1 594 323 classes, 729 shards, ≈ 29 h, ≤ 37 GB; k = 13, 0.8569, ≈ 2¼ h, ≈ 23 GB, with an independent lean4checker replay; k = 12, 0.8476, ≈ 15 min, ≈ 18 GB; k = 17, 0.895, explicitly informal ([msharpe248/collatz](https://github.com/msharpe248/collatz)) **[P, self-reported]**. Its exponents are lower than this project's at equal k because of an "integer-grid weakening" (1/50 grid caps), and it proves KL's difference inequalities and a root-induction growth theorem in Lean rather than taking Theorem 2.2 as an axiom; it also states "no-go" theorems for bounded multiplicative potentials over bounded windows and an odd-step-density statement about hypothetical divergent orbits, and the README has an internal inconsistency (177 147 classes attributed once to k = 13, once to k = 12) **[P]**. Menta2357/collatz-classical (Apache-2.0, created 2026-07-05, 194 commits, Spanish README) reproduces KL 2003's own table at k = 11 with rational λ = 71689/40000, 59 049 rows and 19 683 auxiliary groups, **γ_11 = 0.8417560**, "kernel-clean", and audits Liu's arXiv:2512.13760 (v1 claimed x^{0.946}, v2 retreated to x^{0.3227}, "independent blockers") ([Menta2357](https://github.com/Menta2357/collatz-classical), [Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13760)) **[P, self-reported]**. neljaouh/ConjectureA (Apache-2.0, created 2026-09-25, 0 stars, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23025918) claims Lean proofs of Conjecture A and the growth-exponent conjecture with only the standard axioms, but "rests on one upstream input, `predecessors_positive_lower_density` (M2)" vendored unmodified from ProofAtlas, with Claude Code credited as a substantial contributor ([neljaouh/ConjectureA](https://github.com/neljaouh/ConjectureA)) **[P]**. Since Conjecture A is essentially M2's statement, ConjectureA adds packaging, not mathematics; the real question is whether M2 is sound, and if it were, it would settle Conjecture A, the λ_k → 2 problem and Wirsching's programme at once — which is itself grounds for scepticism pending human review **[inference]**. ccchallenge.org tracks 374 papers, lists KrasikovLagarias2003 as "being formalised" (not formalised) and has Eliahou1993, Hercher2023, RozierTerracol2025 and two Tao2022 formalisations "ready to be audited" ([ccchallenge.org](https://ccchallenge.org/)) **[P]**; whether its K–L entry is Menta2357's is not visible **[U]**. Three implementations (msharpe248, Mazur, this project) agree on finite-k LP values where they overlap **[I]**.

The trust-base tiers are quantified as follows. **Kernel reduction**: msharpe248's 29 h / 37 GB for 1.6 M rows **[P, self-reported]**; Mian–Siddique's kernel-certified Erdős–Mollin–Walsh verification below 10^14 took ≈ 46 CPU-hours with 3 524 certificates and no `native_decide` ([arXiv:2609.25011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25011)) **[P, preprint]**. Extrapolating linearly, kernel checking at k = 20–21 (1.2–3.5·10^9 rows) is three orders of magnitude out of reach **[inference]**. **`native_decide`/reflection**: Lean 4.29.0 represents each native computation "as one axiom per computation" visible in `#print axioms` ([release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/releases/v4.29.0/)) **[P]**, and the tactic documentation warns it "adds the entire lean compiler to the trusted part" ([docs mirror](https://florisvandoorn.com/LeanCourse24/docs/Lean/Parser/Tactic.html)) **[P]**. This project's replication on an M5/24 GB: k = 18 potential 568 s, LP 818 s (Mazur: 876 s / 1111 s); k = 19 (387 420 489 classes) with a tail-recursive `LoopCheck` replacing `List.range N`: potential 1819 s (peak ≈ 7.7 GB), LP 2335 s; memory model ≥ 16 B/class for the list plus 8 B/class each for the `Array Nat` and hex string, hence estimates of ≈ 30 GiB at k = 20 and ≈ 90 GiB at k = 21 with LoopCheck versus ≈ 290 GiB with Mazur's list-based checker **[I]**. **Streamed external verified checkers**: cake_lpr (Tan, Heule, Myreen 2021) is a verified LRAT checker whose machine code is verified through CakeML's toolchain ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-72013-1_12)) **[P]**; the Boolean Pythagorean triples proof (Heule, Kullmann, Marek 2016) was ~200 TB of DRAT, 68 GB compressed, later Coq-checked ([arXiv:1605.00723](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.00723v1)) **[P]**; Keller's conjecture (Brakensiek, Heule, Mackey, Narváez 2020) used formally verified checkers ([arXiv:1910.03740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.03740)) **[P]**; Szeider's lrat-catcher (arXiv:2607.00815, Jul–Sep 2026) streams LRAT certificates into Lean theorems using Lean core's verified checker as compiled native code, with memory depending on the live clause set, and imported **174 TB** of certificates in a scaling experiment ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00815)) **[P, preprint]**; PBLean (arXiv:2602.08692) imports VeriPB pseudo-Boolean certificates by reflection with a Lean-proved checker ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08692)) **[P, preprint]**. No head-to-head benchmark of `native_decide` versus these checkers on the same certificate exists **[P, negative]**. The design implication is direct: a verified LP-certificate checker (correctness proved once in Lean, run natively over a streamed, chunked certificate, as PBLean and lrat-catcher do) would give the `native_decide` trust base with memory constant in N — the plausible route for k = 21, and nothing of the kind currently exists **[inference]**.

The July 2026 kernel episode fixes the minimum standard for any Lean-based Collatz claim. Ramana Kumar published (25 Jul 2026) a `sorry`-free, axiom-free AI-assisted "disproof" of Collatz (repo `xrchz/CollatzLean`) that was a proof of `False` via kernel bug #14576 in nested-inductive handling; Kiran Gopinathan minimised it, the fix landed within an hour of the 28 Jul issue and shipped in **Lean 4.32.2**; the proof had also passed a week-old nanoda because of a separate nanoda bug; de Moura: "This is an implementation bug, not a hole in Lean's meta-theory"; AI-assisted review then found further kernel mistakes ([postmortem, 1 Aug 2026](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-8-1-postmortem-for-kernel-soundness-bug-14576/)) **[P]**. The bug was reachable only via metaprogramming that sends declarations straight to the kernel, which is exactly what large-certificate pipelines do **[P]**. The watch-list therefore reads: `#print axioms`, a toolchain pinned at ≥ 4.32.2, an independent checker (nanoda, lean4lean, lean4checker), and a separate statement-alignment review — ProofAtlas itself flags that "Lean verification doesn't automatically confirm that a paper matches the formal statement" ([proofatlas.ai](https://proofatlas.ai/)) **[P]**.

On the compute side, the verification history that any Lean hypothesis "all n < 2^71 converge" inherits is: Oliveira e Silva 20·2^58 = 5 764 607 523 034 234 880 on 18 Jan 2009 after ≈ 81.1 CPU-years, published in *The Ultimate Challenge* pp. 189–207 ([sweet.ua.pt](http://sweet.ua.pt/tos/3x_plus_1.html)) **[P]**; Roosendaal 2^60 in May 2011 **[P]**; yoyo@home 87·2^60 ≈ 1.003·10^20 in 2017 with ~1 000 volunteers and **each number independently checked twice** ([Barina PDF](https://www.fit.vut.cz/research/publication-file/c197809/298514/s11227-025-07337-0.pdf), [ericr.nl](https://www.ericr.nl/wondrous/)) **[P]**; Barina 2^68 (2020) and 2^71 (2025) with work units of 2^40 numbers, a server managing 2^32 units (room to 2^72), OpenCL on NVIDIA and AMD **[P]**. Above 87·2^60 the range is single-implementation (Barina's code) with no stated double-check policy **[P for the page; inference for the trust consequence]**; the frontier moves at roughly one bit per 2–3 years, and the cycle corollaries move with it. Modest refereed 2025–2026 results worth tracking: Knight, "Collatz high cycles do not exist", Discrete Math. 349 (2026) 114812 ([HAL PDF](https://hal.science/hal-04261183/file/main.pdf)) **[P]**; Eliahou–Verger-Gaugry, "The number system in rational base 3/2 and the 3x+1 problem", C. R. Math. 363 (2025) 329–336, DOI 10.5802/crmath.662 ([C. R. Math.](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mathematique/articles/10.5802/crmath.662/)) **[P]**. Nothing in 2025–2026 changed the conjecture's status; Althöfer's prize page still states a formal proof is missing ([althofer.de](https://althofer.de/collatz-prizes.html)) **[P]**, and no Isabelle AFP or Coq Collatz library exists **[P, negative]**.

## Conclusion

Three things change how the program should present itself. First, the competitive field at finite k is now entirely computational and unrefereed: 0.8676 (kernel, k = 14), 0.90 (`native_decide`, k = 18) and 0.9203 (this project, k = 21) are all claims in the same sense, and the refereed record of 0.84 will remain the citable one until a human-reviewed venue accepts a certificate; the natural venues are the ccchallenge.org audit pipeline, where a K–L 2003 formalization is already being audited, and a short refereed note that registers the k = 21 certificate's sha256 and the external int64/mpmath verifiers. Second, the asymptotic question λ_k → 2 is untouched in print since 2003, and the project's transfer-operator reading of it on Z_3 has no competitor — but the stakes have shifted, because Mazur's unrefereed positive-density theorem M2, if sound, would make every x^γ statement obsolete as a statement while leaving explicit certified exponents as the only checkable content; whether a human expert has digested M2 is the single most important open question in this landscape. Third, the formal-verification toolchain now carries a documented failure mode (kernel bug #14576, exploited through exactly the metaprogramming path large certificates use), so the k = 21 Lean story needs a streamed, verified LP-certificate checker in the lrat-catcher/PBLean style plus an independent checker, rather than ever-larger `include_str` payloads.

The long-shot strands sort cleanly by expected value. The 5x+1 exponent is the best: no explicit c1 exists, the LP machinery transfers with 5^k residues, and a result above 0.651 would adjudicate between two published heuristics. The Terras check is cheap but must start past Rozier–Terracol's 2.8·10^19 and use Barina's 2^71 to be new. The 3x+d catalogue offers only data (the density LP is isomorphic to d = 1) and the rewriting strand promises negative results. On cycles, the frontier belongs to verification range, not theory: the next semiconvergent pair needs roughly 2^75 under Eliahou-type arguments and Hercher-type residue analysis buys about 1.3 bits, so a block-DFS that wins 2–3 bits would convert Barina's current 2^71 into K ≥ q_24 = 753 110 839 881 — a concrete, publishable target that no one has stated in print.
